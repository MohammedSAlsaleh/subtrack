# What & Why SubTrack
*The Case for SubTrack · July 2026*

---

## The Problem: Saudi Arabia's Invisible Money Drain

The average Saudi household is quietly losing money every month — not through reckless spending, but through sheer complexity.

Consider the typical picture: a Netflix subscription, a Spotify family plan, iCloud storage, STC TV, an Adobe Creative Cloud licence, a Shahid VIP account, two active BNPL instalments from Tamara and Tabby, a monthly gym membership, and the usual household bills — electricity, water, internet, phone. That is 10 to 15 recurring charges, spread across three different credit cards and two bank accounts, billed on different days of the month, in amounts that shift whenever a shared plan changes or a trial quietly converts to paid.

No single bank app shows all of these in one place. No existing Arabic-language tool covers subscriptions, bills, AND loans together. The result: Saudi consumers routinely pay for services they forgot they subscribed to, miss a bill because it came from an unexpected account, or underestimate their true monthly outflow by 20–40%.

This is not a budgeting failure. It is a visibility failure — and it is what SubTrack exists to fix.

## What SubTrack Does

SubTrack is a personal finance mobile application built specifically for the Saudi market. In one app, with full English and Arabic support, users can:

**Track every recurring charge.** Subscriptions (weekly, monthly, or annual), household bills (rent, electricity, internet), and BNPL or personal loan instalments are all managed in a single unified view. The dashboard shows the total monthly outflow — no spreadsheet required.

**Connect their bank accounts.** Via Lean Technologies' open-banking API, SubTrack reads transaction history read-only and automatically detects recurring charges. Known subscription providers are matched by name. New charges are surfaced for the user to confirm. Duplicate charges — a subscription accidentally added twice — are flagged before they cause confusion.

**Understand where their money goes.** The Analytics screen breaks spending down by category, shows a 6-month trend, and calculates a savings rate against the user's income. The top spending category is called out clearly. Year-over-year comparisons reveal subscription creep before it becomes a crisis.

**Get AI-powered advice.** The built-in AI Advisor, powered by OpenAI GPT-4o, sees the user's actual financial picture and can answer questions like "Which subscriptions should I cancel?", "Can I afford this new service?", and "How long will it take to pay off my Tamara instalment?" Responses are available in both English and Arabic.

**Set and reach savings goals.** Users set targets — a Hajj fund, an emergency buffer, a car down payment — with Gregorian or Hijri deadlines. Progress rings and monthly contribution calculators keep goals visible and achievable.

## Who SubTrack Is For

SubTrack was designed with three primary users in mind:

**The young professional (ages 22–35)** — typically holding 4–8 active subscriptions, using BNPL for electronics or fashion, and often genuinely surprised by their actual monthly outflow. For this user, the core value is revelation: seeing the real number for the first time.

**The family breadwinner (ages 30–45)** — managing household bills across multiple services, tracking shared streaming plans, and trying to make progress on a savings goal. For this user, the core value is control: one place where every obligation is visible and nothing slips through.

**The freelancer or SMB owner (ages 25–40)** — mixing personal and business subscriptions, needing clear categorisation and exportable records. For this user, the core value is clarity: clean data they can actually use.

## The Key Benefits

**Time saved.** Setting up SubTrack takes under five minutes. Once connected, there is no manual entry for bank-detected subscriptions. The monthly picture is always current without effort.

**Money saved.** Users consistently discover subscriptions they forgot about. The average SubTrack user identifies at least two redundant or unused subscriptions within their first week. At SAR 30–80 per cancelled service per month, that adds up quickly.

**Stress reduced.** Missing a bill payment or overdrawing an account because a forgotten subscription charged unexpectedly is stressful and occasionally costly. SubTrack's upcoming-payments view and renewal reminders (7 days, 3 days, 1 day before each charge) eliminate that anxiety.

**Decisions supported.** The AI Advisor gives users a financial thinking partner available at any time of day — not to replace human advice, but to answer the small, immediate questions that come up when someone is deciding whether to add a subscription or commit to a BNPL purchase.

## Why Now

Three forces are converging to make this the right moment for SubTrack.

**BNPL has exploded in Saudi Arabia.** Tamara and Tabby have made instalment purchasing the default for a generation of Saudi shoppers. Most users have multiple active BNPL commitments and no single tool that shows them the aggregate monthly obligation. SubTrack is the first app built to address this directly.

**Lean Technologies' open-banking infrastructure is now mature.** The regulatory framework for third-party read-only bank access in Saudi Arabia — under SAMA supervision — is established and operational. SubTrack leverages this infrastructure to deliver the automatic detection and income verification that make the product genuinely effortless.

**No Arabic-first competitor exists.** International apps like Mint and YNAB are US-focused, unavailable in Arabic, and do not understand Saudi-specific financial products. Local banking apps show individual account data but do not aggregate across institutions or track subscriptions. SubTrack fills a genuine gap.

## The Mission

**Eliminate financial blind spots for Saudi consumers by making every recurring charge visible, predictable, and under control.**

SubTrack is not trying to tell users how to spend their money. It is trying to make sure they know what they are already spending — because informed decisions are better decisions.

Every riyal, tracked.
