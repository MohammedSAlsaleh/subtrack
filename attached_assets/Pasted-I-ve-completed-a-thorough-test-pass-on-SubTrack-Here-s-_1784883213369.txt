I've completed a thorough test pass on SubTrack. Here's what I found, organized by severity.

**Functional bugs**

The clearest bug is a date calculation error for monthly subscriptions added through the main "Add Subscription" form: entering a start date of July 24, 2026 produced a "Next Billing" of August 23, 2026 (30 days later) instead of August 24 (a true one-month cycle). I confirmed this is inconsistent by adding a subscription through the "Suggested Subscriptions" quick-add button instead, which correctly calculated August 24 for the same July 24 start date — so the two add-flows use different date math.

Every subscription I inspected (including brand-new ones created today) shows four months of "Payment History" stretching into the past, even for a subscription that was just created with a start date of today. This makes the payment history feel like generic placeholder data rather than tied to the actual subscription record.

Throughout the app, overdue items are labeled with negative day counts like "In -6 days" and "In -2 days" instead of something like "Overdue by 6 days." This appears on the dashboard, subscriptions list, and detail pages consistently.

Clicking the notification bell icon on the dashboard navigates straight to the Settings page rather than opening any kind of notifications panel — the icon and behavior don't match.

On page load, the dashboard briefly greets the user with "Good morning SubTrack" before correcting itself to "Good morning claude" once the profile data loads — a flash-of-placeholder-content issue.

**UI/layout bugs**

On the Subscriptions page, the category filter pills (Streaming, Software, etc.) render correctly as small horizontal pills normally, but stretch into tall, empty vertical boxes whenever the filtered list is short — for example when searching for "Netflix" or viewing the "Cancelled" tab (which has only one item). This looks like a flexbox stretching bug tied to list length.

**Design observations (not necessarily bugs)**

The Bills, Loans, and Category Budgets pages use a premium paywall that dims the underlying content rather than blurring or hiding it — actual dollar amounts and account details remain legible behind the "Unlock with Premium" overlay, which may undercut the intended paywall.

**What worked well**

Core CRUD operations (add, edit, delete subscriptions), the savings goal contribution flow, form validation with clear inline error messages, search and category filtering, the light/dark/system theme toggle, and mobile responsiveness all worked correctly and consistently. Math checked out everywhere I verified it (category totals, savings percentages, monthly-to-yearly conversions).

I made one temporary test entry ("Test Gym Membership") and one temporary income change during testing, both of which I've cleaned up/reverted, so the dummy data is back to its original state.
